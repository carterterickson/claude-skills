# Encounter Building & Stat Block Design (2024 Ruleset)

This party has **6 PCs**, currently level 6-7, aiming toward level 15+. The 2024 DMG's encounter-building guidance is calibrated around a party of 4. Everything here adjusts for a 6-PC table.

## Encounter difficulty math

Use your knowledge of the 2024 DMG's encounter-building system (XP budget by party level and target difficulty — Low/Moderate/High) as the starting point:

1. Determine target difficulty for the scene's narrative purpose (a random travel encounter should usually be Low-Moderate; a boss fight or climax should be High).
2. Calculate the XP budget for a 4-PC party at the party's current level per that difficulty band.
3. **Scale for 6 PCs**: don't just multiply the budget by 1.5 — that tends to overwhelm players with too many bodies to track and slows the table down. Prefer:
   - Adding 1-2 additional standard monsters rather than proportionally inflating everything, OR
   - Bumping a couple of monsters up a CR tier, OR
   - Adding a boss's legendary actions/extra lair actions to give it more "turns" against more PCs, rather than just adding raw HP.
4. Sanity-check action economy: with 6 PCs, a fight can end in one round if the enemy count is too low relative to player action economy. As a rule of thumb, err toward slightly more enemies (or one tougher boss with legendary actions) over slightly fewer — six characters produce a lot of actions per round.
5. If genuinely unsure of exact current XP threshold numbers, do a quick web search to confirm current 2024 DMG figures rather than guessing — encounter math errors either trivialize a set-piece or accidentally kill a PC.

## Building the encounter roster

- Prefer **1 notable threat + a handful of standard monsters** over "one big bag of HP" or "a wall of identical mooks" — it gives players more tactical texture and makes positioning/target-prioritization matter.
- Vary monster roles (a brute, a controller/caster, some fodder, maybe a support/buffer) rather than reusing the same statblock six times.
- Terrain and objectives matter as much as the roster — note 1-2 terrain features or environmental hazards/opportunities per combat that give players non-attack options (cover, hazards to exploit, something to interact with).

## Standard monster stat blocks (Monster Manual 2024)

- Select creatures by CR appropriate to the difficulty math above and the fictional/thematic fit.
- Write the full stat block out using your own knowledge of the creature's 2024 mechanics (AC, HP, speed, abilities, actions, etc.) — this is fine to do in full since stat block mechanics are functional game data, not literary prose to reproduce verbatim.
- Reflavor freely: renaming a monster, changing its described appearance/origin, or reskinning its "fluff" to fit the homebrew world is encouraged and keeps a Monster Manual creature from feeling generic — just keep the mechanics intact unless there's a specific reason to houserule them.
- Note the creature's CR and source next to its name in the document (e.g., "Owlbear (MM 2024, CR 3) — reskinned as a Duskwood Stalker") so the DM can look up the original if needed.

## Homebrewed bosses & important named foes

Named/boss enemies deserve unique stat blocks — a reskinned standard monster rarely feels climactic. Use 2024 DMG monster-design guidance as a mental model:

- **Anchor to a CR/level target** using the same difficulty math above, then design abilities that fit that power budget rather than picking numbers arbitrarily.
- Give a true boss (a solo enemy expected to fight all 6 PCs alone) some way to act more than once per round and resist single-target lockdown — legendary actions (2-3 minor actions usable on other PCs' turns) and legendary resistance (a few auto-succeeds on failed saves per day) are the standard 2024 toolkit for this.
- Consider lair actions if the fight has a signature location — a boss's home turf doing something on initiative count 20 each round adds texture without adding another full turn to track.
- Give the boss **one signature mechanic** that makes the fight memorable and distinct from a normal monster fight — a phase change, an environmental interaction, a mechanic tied to the plot thread this encounter serves, or a weakness players can discover/exploit through roleplay or investigation, not just damage output.
- For a boss fought alongside minions (not solo), you can scale back the legendary-action toolkit and instead make the minions' presence the "extra actions" — simpler to design and run.
- Multiattack and damage should target "bloodied around the midpoint of the expected fight" for a boss with a phase change, so the shift lands with narrative weight rather than at a random moment.

## Non-combat "encounters" (social, political, chase, stealth)

These deserve the same rigor as combat:

- **Social/political**: Define each key NPC's want, their leverage/information, and what specific player approaches would change their stance. Note suggested DCs for key social checks (Persuasion/Deception/Intimidation/Insight), and what happens on both success and failure — failure should complicate, rarely just end the scene.
- **Chase**: Use a simple structured chase (a handful of "legs," each requiring a check or presenting an obstacle/choice) rather than freeform description, so pacing stays tight. Note complications that can trigger mid-chase (obstacles, NPCs joining, environmental hazards) and how player choices (route, spells, tactics) shortcut or complicate it.
- **Stealth/infiltration**: Map out the guarded area's key obstacles (patrols, locks, alarms, witnesses) and give multiple viable approaches (brute force, social pretext, magical, environmental). Note DCs for key checks and what discovery actually triggers — rarely "instant combat," more interestingly "raises the stakes."

Whatever the type, the same "objective, stakes, branch point" structure from the main SKILL.md applies — combat is just one flavor of encounter, not the only one that needs planning rigor.
